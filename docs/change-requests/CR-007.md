## 2. Abuse reports

### 2.1 Only an encounter can be reported

A report carries the reported identity's own signature from the nearby
session it came from (its HELLO), and, after a match, the conversation id.
The backend accepts a report only if that signature verifies, so a person can
be reported only by someone who actually met them. Message content goes with
a report only through the consented evidence bundle (SRS-SAF-004), kept
apart from telemetry (TRS-BCK-014).

### 2.2 A weighted score, built to resist brigading

The score is the decayed sum of **distinct** reporters' weights, never a raw
count. A reporter's weight rises with:

- the age of the install;
- a genuine-device result (Play Integrity / App Attest; a new dependency,
  approved when applied);
- a history of reports that were upheld.

It falls for:

- bursts of reports from new installs;
- reporters who never exchanged a message with the person;
- reporters whose past reports were rejected.

Weights, decay and thresholds are register values.

### 2.3 Escalation ladder: owner ruling, 2026-11-23, "limits + temporary suspension"

| Step | Trigger | Automatic? | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Score ≥ limit threshold | Yes | Quiet limits: smaller discovery reach, fewer interests per hour |
| 2 | Score ≥ suspension threshold | Yes | Temporary suspension (default to be set, e.g. 72 h), with a notice and an appeal (SRS-SAF-018) |
| 3 | Reviewed evidence | **No: a human decides** | Permanent ban (SRS-SAF-015) |
| — | A credible "may be a minor" or threat report | Yes | Profile hidden from discovery at once, pending human review |

The system never issues a permanent ban automatically. That is the owner's
ruling, because a coordinated false-report campaign against an innocent
user, including LGBTQIA users, must never be able to remove them for good.

### 2.4 Enforcement that works offline

- The backend signs a revocation, and the relay refuses that identity's
  registration and envelopes (SRS-IDN-008).
- A compact **signed denylist** of revoked identity-key hashes rides the
  normal config refresh. Every phone refuses HELLO from a listed identity in
  nearby discovery, even with no network at the time.
- The suspended app shows a suspension screen with the appeal path the next
  time it reaches the backend.
- **Known limit:** reinstalling creates a new identity. Device attestation
  raises the cost of evasion; only identity verification would stop it.

## 3. Minor protection

### 3.1 Owner ruling, 2026-11-23: store-compliant on both stores

The owner chose whatever mechanism keeps the app approved and in good
standing on Google Play and the App Store. As researched on 2026-11-23:

- **Google Play.** The Age-Restricted Content and Functionality policy
  requires dating and matchmaking apps to use **Restrict Minor Access** in
  Play Console. Dating apps must also meet the **Child Safety Standards**
  policy. Google does not mandate a particular age-gating technology.
- **App Store.** From 2026-11-16, apps that declare **Dating and
  Relationships** in the age-rating questionnaire get a minimum **18+**
  rating, with declarations due for all apps by 2027-01-12. An 18+ rating
  does not by itself require independent age verification. In regions where
  the law requires it, Apple provides age categories through the **Declared
  Age Range** API.

### 3.2 What Lumos does

1. **Store configuration (release checklist, owner actions).**
   - Google Play: Restrict Minor Access on, and the Child Safety Standards
     declaration completed (published standards, in-app reporting, CSAM
     handling, a child-safety contact).
   - App Store: Dating and Relationships declared, so the app is rated 18+.
2. **In-app 18+ gate** (SRS-ONB-001), with no way back in on the same
   install after an under-18 answer.
3. **Platform age signals where the law or the platform provides them.**
   These are Google Play Age Signals and Apple Declared Age Range, used only
   to block under-18 users, which is the only use the terms allow. They are
   gated per market by configuration (SRS-NFR-LEG-007). Each is a new
   dependency with terms to accept, so it needs owner approval at the slice.
4. **On-device screening of profile and intent text.** Text stating an
   under-18 age or a school context is refused. This uses the CR-006 rule
   engine and adds no dependency.
5. **"May be a minor" report.** One credible report hides the profile at
   once, pending review (§2.3).
6. **Predator signal.** An adult who sent interest to a profile later
   confirmed as a minor is queued for review; repeat cases lead to a ban.
7. **No vendor age verification in this CR.** A selfie or ID check is not a
   store requirement. It stays an option for markets whose law demands it,
   behind SRS-NFR-LEG-007's per-market gate.

### 3.3 Known limit

A self-declared 18+ gate can be lied past. Platform signals, screening and
reports reduce that risk; they cannot remove it. The release notes and the
privacy policy must not claim otherwise.
