# CR-006: Mutual-compatibility visibility in discovery, and offline free-text intent matching

- **Status:** **ACCEPTED** by the owner on 2026-11-23. The owner raised the
  requirement and ruled every open point the same day: the text matcher
  (§6), undisclosed gender (§3), timing, the whole §5 safety set, the
  matching-gender list (§3), and Discover ads following the same rule (§8).
  Nothing here is applied yet.
- **Executes:** after WP-05 closes (owner ruling, 2026-11-23), as its own
  slices on Android and iOS alternately (the 2026-11-23 ordering ruling).
- **Raised:** 2026-11-23 by the owner. Recorded by the implementing agent
  because it changes discovery behaviour the SRS fixes, and is expected to
  add a message class and register values (TRS-PROTO-016, TRS-CONST-003).
- **Affected requirements:** SRS-NBY-011 (local filtering by compatible
  intent; this CR makes it mutual and mandatory), SRS-PRO-001/002/003
  (gender, orientation and intent fields; inclusive values; per-field
  visibility), SRS-NFR-PRI-002 (nothing sensitive in BLE advertisements),
  SRS-NFR-PRI-005 and SRS-DAT-006 (no orientation or preference in analytics
  or telemetry), SRS-MAT-001 (interest only to an eligible peer),
  SRS-SAF-001/002/003 (block, hide and report), SRS-NBY-012 (hide without
  notifying).
- **ADRs:** a new ADR will record the compatibility exchange's design when the
  slice starts; ADR-002 (the nearby session is the trust point) and ADR-004
  (tunable values come from the register) apply.

## 1. The requirement, in the owner's words

> Assume in my profile I say I am a man and I seek woman AND men both then I
> should only see ADs by women who seek men and Men who seek Men and nothing
> else. Same criteria goes for all possible combinations including LGBTQIA.
> [...] ADs and visibility should not be random and open for anyone which can
> cause bully or harassments.

> There should be also a filter where I can write anything random like
> "Seeking women who wants to travel to ParkStreet in Cab Share" and if any
> women wants same [...] we should be able to see each other. [...] we would be
> needing some small fully offline AI filter inside iOS/Android App [...] But
> lightweight very lightweight and compatible with iOS Android.

The stated aims are no noise, no exposure to people who are not interested,
and no route for bullying or harassment, especially of LGBTQIA users.

## 2. Why this is a change request

SRS-NBY-011 (P1) lets a user filter the cards **they** see. It is one-sided
and optional. Nothing stops a user's own card from reaching people who are not
looking for someone like them. The owner's requirement makes visibility
**mutual and mandatory**, which changes what the discovery flow exchanges
before a card is shown. It is also expected to need a new pre-preview message
class and new register values.

## 3. The visibility rule

Only two fields are needed: the user's **matching gender** and their
**seeking set**. An orientation label is never used for matching; orientation
follows from these two fields, and SRS-PRO-002's orientation field stays a
display field under the user's visibility control.

- **Matching genders (owner ruling, 2026-11-23):** `woman`, `man`,
  `non-binary`. The profile can still show any self-described identity; this
  list only says which seeking choices a profile answers to.
- **Seeking set:** any non-empty subset of the matching genders, or
  `everyone`.
- **Rule:** A and B are shown to each other **only if** A's matching gender is
  in B's seeking set (or B seeks `everyone`) **and** B's matching gender is in
  A's seeking set (or A seeks `everyone`). The rule is symmetric, so both
  phones always reach the same answer.
- **Undisclosed or self-described gender (owner ruling, 2026-11-23):** a
  profile whose gender is "prefer not to say", or self-described with no
  matching gender, is shown **only to people who seek `everyone`**. That user
  sees only people who would accept them, so in practice also only
  `everyone` seekers. Nothing is guessed.

Worked example, as the owner gave it. A man seeking women and men sees women
seeking men and men seeking men; men seeking `everyone`; and women seeking
`everyone`. He does not see women who seek only women, men who seek only
women, or non-binary people of any kind (he does not seek non-binary
people).

## 4. Where the check runs, and what leaks

- **Never in the BLE advertisement.** SRS-NFR-PRI-002 already forbids
  gender and orientation there, and this CR keeps that.
- **Inside the authenticated, encrypted nearby session, before any preview.**
  The two phones run a compatibility exchange right after HELLO/HELLO_ACK.
  PROFILE_PREVIEW (alias, photo, intent text) is sent **only** when the
  answer is "compatible". An incompatible peer receives no card and is told
  nothing beyond "no card".
- **Blinded, not plain.** Sending each other's gender and seeking set in the
  clear, even encrypted, would hand both to any stranger in radio range.
  Because the value space is tiny, plain hashing hides nothing. The ADR will
  pick a construction whose only output to each side is the yes/no bit, such
  as a two-party set-membership check over the session keys. That choice,
  with its vectors on all three languages, is the main engineering risk of
  this CR.
- **The inherent limit, stated plainly.** A "yes" necessarily tells B that
  A's gender is in B's seeking set and that A would accept B's gender. That
  is the purpose of the feature. A user who **lies** about their own gender
  or seeking set still sees the people that lie matches. No protocol can
  prevent that without identity verification, which is why §5 exists.
- **Telemetry and analytics:** none of these fields, results or counts
  leave the phone (SRS-NFR-PRI-005, SRS-DAT-006).

## 5. Safety set: ACCEPTED by the owner, 2026-11-23

Mutual filtering hides users from honest strangers, not from liars. The
implementing agent proposes pairing it with the following, all of which
work offline:

1. **No sensitive data in the broadcast; a yes/no bit only in the session**
   (§4).
2. **Discreet mode.** The user can turn it on, and it is suggested during
   onboarding. Photo and alias stay blurred until **both** sides have sent
   interest. A sweeping liar then sees only a blurred card and an intent
   line.
3. **Rate limits from the register.** These cap distinct cards evaluated per
   hour, interests sent per hour, and how often the matching gender or
   seeking set can be changed (a cooldown). The cooldown stops a user from
   flipping their profile to probe a venue.
4. **Block and report work fully offline** (report enforcement is CR-007). A block is immediate and
   permanent on the device. A report queues, includes evidence only with
   consent (SRS-SAF-004), and is sent later without chat content.
5. **On-device abuse filter for intent text.** Slurs and threats are refused
   on the writer's phone before anyone sees them. Intent text travels only
   inside PROFILE_PREVIEW, so only compatible peers ever read it.
6. **No rejection signals.** There is no "seen", no "passed" and no count of
   viewers. An incompatible or declined peer cannot tell which happened
   (consistent with SRS-NBY-012).

Optional and later: an identity-verification trust badge ("show me only to
verified people"). It needs a server and a vendor, so it is not proposed
now.

## 6. Free-text intent matching

**Owner ruling, 2026-11-23: rules first, model later.** Version 1 adds no
new dependency and gives identical results on Android and iOS. A small
on-device model can be added later behind the same interface, and only with
its own dependency approval (name, version, license and size stated first).

Version 1 matcher, run on each phone over the peer's intent text once the
peer is compatible:

1. **Normalise:** Unicode NFKC, lower-case, punctuation to spaces, collapse
   and trim whitespace. **Equal strings match.** This is the owner's exact
   case-insensitive, whitespace-trimmed rule.
2. **Strip the gender phrase** ("seeking women who", "looking for men",
   ...). Gender is already handled by §3, so "Seeking women who wants to
   travel to ParkStreet in Cab Share" and "Seeking men who wants to travel to
   ParkStreet in Cab Share" become the same text and match.
3. **Canonical tokens:** drop stop-words, then map through one shared table
   of synonyms and joined place names (`cab`/`taxi`/`uber`/`ride` →
   `cab`; `park street`/`parkstreet` → `parkstreet`; `share`/`pool` →
   `share`).
4. **Similarity:** compute the overlap of the two token sets and compare it
   to a register threshold. The check is symmetric, so both phones agree.

The stop-word, gender-phrase and synonym tables are **generated register
data**, emitted to Java and Swift like the constants. Golden vectors list
text pairs with the expected match, and run on Go, Java and Swift.
English-only in version 1.

## 7. Register changes this CR expects (values set when applied)

- A message class for the compatibility exchange, joining the consent
  gate's pre-preview set.
- `discovery.COMPAT_*` rate limits and the profile-change cooldown (§5.3).
- `discovery.INTENT_MATCH_MIN_SIMILARITY` and the matcher tables (§6).

Each value is stated with its default and range in the applying slice, and
the slice regenerates `constants_hash` and every vector that embeds it
(TRS-CONST-003).

## 8. Discover ads (owner ruling, 2026-11-23)

Discover ads (WP-08) follow the same mutual rule. An ad is shown only to people
its author would accept and who would accept the author, evaluated the same
way and with the same yes/no-only disclosure as nearby cards.
